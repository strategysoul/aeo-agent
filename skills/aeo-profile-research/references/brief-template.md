# Research brief template

Write `research/brief.md` in this shape. Keep each section to what the later steps need: keywords, AI questions, and recommendations. Every bullet ends with its source and date.

```markdown
# Research brief: <domain>

Last updated: <YYYY-MM-DD> · run <run_id>
Countries: <...> · Languages: <...>
Approval: see state.json

## Coverage

| # | Question | Status | Last evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Product and price | answered | 2026-10-17 |
...

## 1. Product and price
- <finding> — [source](<URL>), <date>

## 2. Audiences, their goals and the words they use

### <Audience name>
- Goals: <...> — [source](<URL>), <date>
- Words they use: "<quote>", "<quote>" — [source](<URL>), <date>

## 3. Competitors and alternatives

| Competitor | Type | In top 10 for | Positioning | Price | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Competitor ads (Google Ads Transparency Center, <region>, last 30 days):
- <competitor>: sponsored results on <n> of <n> starting searches; <n> ads; offers and claims: <...>; names: <products, audiences> — [ads](<URL>), <date>

Alternatives to buying anything: <...>

## 4. Who ranks and gets cited

| Domain | Type | In top 10 for | Cited in AI answers | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Searches showing an AI Overview: <n> of <n checked>.

## 5. Questions people ask
- <question> — [source](<URL>), <date>

## 6. Where audiences read and talk

| Place | Audience | Type | Source |
|---|---|---|---|

## 7. Rules that limit what the business can say
- <rule, what it limits, which country> — [source](<URL>), <date>
- or: No specific rules found for <industry> in <countries>; looked in <places>.

## Still not found
- <question / gap> — looked in <places>
```

Labels: put `(inferred)` after any finding with `method: inferred`, and `(user research, <date>)` after findings taken from the user's own files without a public URL.
